Show all bar levels
How this comparison works

Both sides are expected bars per base ore, using the community Transmuter calculator's formulas. Bar level mode compares one ore tier. Void Drone floor mode starts with the floor's natural ore mix, applies portal upgrades, and compares the resulting mix.

  • Craft multiplier combines the free-craft chance with the 2x, 3x, 5x, 10x, 20x, and 100x craft chances.
  • Bomb of Plenty multiplies the affected ore, applies the natural and BoP golden-ore chances, then crafts all of that ore into bars.
  • Transmuter turns the affected ore directly into bars, can also apply a BoP stack, and the remaining ore is crafted normally.
  • Void Drone floors use the floor's 80/20 or 50/50 ore pair. Each native ore has the portal chance to become a portal; a portaled ore becomes one uniformly random ore from Tin up to its own level and is multiplied by the portal multiplier.
  • Breakpoint is the effective ore cost per bar where the two totals are equal. Cheaper bars favour Bomb of Plenty; more expensive bars favour Transmuter.
  • Bar output multiplier applies to bars from crafting and from the Transmuter Bomb, so it changes the bar totals but not the winner or breakpoint.

Bar costs use the wiki's current values through Neopolium (World 4). Effective cost is max(1, floor(base cost × craft-cost multiplier)), following the community calculator with a minimum of 1 ore per bar; that rounding can move a level right at the breakpoint to the other side. Floor mode is an aggregate bar-count proxy: a mixed floor produces several bar types, so use the native-ore rows for a specific target bar. Golden ore and BoP interactions are expected values, not guaranteed outcomes. Using both bombs together (for example with a spoon auto-firing both) is always better than using either one alone. 3. Estimate

How this estimate works

Goal ETA turns the amount still missing into time at your measured ore per hour. Everything is an estimate, not a promise.

  • Ore goal: ore remaining = max(0, target − current ore), then play hours = ore remaining ÷ ore per hour.
  • Bar goal: bars per ore = bar output multiplier × craft multiplier ÷ effective ore cost. The ore still to mine is the ore needed for the remaining bars, minus the ore you already have.
  • Calendar time: calendar days = play hours ÷ play hours per day. The finish date assumes you spread those hours evenly from now.
  • Deadline: available play time is the wall-clock time before the deadline scaled by your hours per day. The required rate is the ore remaining divided by that play time.
  • Milestones: 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% of the target, with the same rate and hours-per-day assumptions.

Caveats: ore per hour varies by world, pickaxe, rock layout, upgrades, and how actively you play. Bar conversion ignores ore spent on other bars, bombs, or upgrades, and the craft multipliers are random, so short sessions can differ a lot from the average. A deadline assumes you actually play the hours you entered. Current ore and bar balances are not in ExportStats, so keep those fields up to date yourself.

How this planner works

Every contract upgrade is mapped to the goals it helps. The goal value is the product of its upgrades' factors, and a buy is scored by the weighted log gain it adds per Contract Point spent.

  • Additive multi: f = 1 + X × level. Contract bonuses are treated as their own factor, not added into the export's huge stat totals.
  • Chance: f = 1 + min(1, c0 + X × level) × (m − 1), where c0 = export chance − X × owned levels. A chance at 100% gains nothing and reads "Capped".
  • Craft: the triple and 10x craft chances are changed one at a time through the bomb tool's craft multiplier.
  • Bar cost: f = 1 / (1 − 0.05 × level).
  • Per contract: f = 1 + 0.01 × level × contracts completed.
  • Score: ∑ weight × ln(G(L+1) / G(L)) / cost. The plan spends greedily on the best affordable score until no positive buy fits.
  • Respec: budget = spare CP + the cost of every owned level. Owned levels are refunded at the current cost-reduction rate.

Costs use the wiki's CostTable values through 14 extra cap levels. The cost reduction is applied as cost × (1 − contract_upgrade_cost_reduction), which is an assumption worth checking against one in-game price. Portal and damage factors multiply the whole goal, which overstates them when only part of your income uses portals or when pickaxe/bomb use is not 50/50. The two damage branches are averaged 50/50. Greedy spend is not guaranteed to be the exact optimum.

How this works

A port of the community sheet IOM – Best Floor To Farm (V3.4.2, game v2.2.52). Every floor gets a value per hour and floors are ranked by it.

  • Floor ores: floor f mixes ore round(f/2) + max(0, ⌊(f−13)/6⌋) and the next one, 80/20 on odd floors and 50/50 on even ones.
  • Floors per hour: 2880 × base game speed × floor speed modifier, with World 3 at 0.7× until the speed notice is bought and World 4 at 1 − quest modifier. The game speed in your export is divided by the penalties of the floor you exported on.
  • Floor multi: golden ore × ore income × golden floor × rainbow floor × galactic floor × prismatic floor, using the fixed golden-floor chance and modifiers of the “negative” floors (e.g. golden floors at 30–50%, Hattium / Mintium floors cutting portal chance to 0.4×).
  • Void Drone: a portal turns a rock into a random ore of equal or lower level, so it can reach your ore from any higher floor. Auto uses it when its best floor beats the best no-void floor.
  • Value: the share of a “perfect floor” where every ore on screen is yours. Values with and without the Void Drone use different perfect floors, so compare floors, not the percentages across modes.
  • Veins: veins per screen come from your spawn rate and the vein’s rarity (doubled for 2× research, capped at a full screen); void portals can also turn rocks into veins.

Differences from the sheet: stats are read from your saved account instead of the JSON tab, hand edits are kept as overrides, every reminder is shown (not just the first), the “can’t one-shot” check reports the real answer where the sheet shows #VALUE! and then says “yes”, and Void Drone can be forced on or off. Warning: the sheet’s numbers assume the stats are exported on a floor with no modifiers.
