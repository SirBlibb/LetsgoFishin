Legendary Fish Average time to catch one

7. All docks

The same setup compared across every dock. Times assume the fisher and every fishing drone are assigned to the selected dock.

How this estimate works

A dock rolls for its legendary fish only when its tick meter fills, and only after the dock is eligible. The tool turns your fishing stats into ticks per second, dock fills per hour, and a legendary chance per fill, then uses the geometric distribution for the average and the percentile times.

  • Dock power: (rod power + drones × drone base × drone multiplier) × Tier 2 dock power. The Tier 2 multiplier only applies to Cave, Volcano, Sky, Solaris, and Galaxy.
  • Catch chance: 100 × dock power ÷ last-fish power rating, capped at 999%.
  • Legendary steps: min(9, floor(catch chance / 100)). All four polychrome cards are required. Each step gives a 1/150,000 base chance per dock fill.
  • Angler bonus: while equipped and fuelled, 1 + 0.02 × (grade + 1) multiplies the legendary chance, so 150,000 becomes about 81,000 at the highest grade.
  • Ticks per event: double, triple, and 5x tick procs are independent and stack (2×3×5× = 30 ticks). The expected ticks per tick-bar fill is the fishing optimizer's own formula.
  • Angler ticks: the suit gives 2 ticks per 1140 − 40 × suit level second cycle while equipped. While fuelled it also has a 1% chance per cycle of 6 × (grade + 1) extra ticks, so the expected Angler rate is (2 + 1% × 6 × (grade + 1)) ÷ cycle seconds.
  • Ticks per second: ticks per event ÷ tick bar seconds + Angler passive ticks. Dock fills per second is ticks per second divided by the ticks required for that dock.
  • Average catch time: 1 ÷ (dock fills per second × legendary chance per fill). Median and 90%/95%/99% times use the geometric distribution, not a simple multiplication.

Assumptions: every fishing drone and the fisher are on the selected dock; game speed does not affect normal fishing ticks (Angler drone ticks are treated as real-time); offline ticks are identical to online; tick overflow carries into the next dock fill; each full 100% of catch chance is one step (so 199% is still 1/150,000); and the Angler stays equipped and fuelled. The 3x tick buff is temporary, so leave it off for a long-run average. The result is a long-run average, not a guarantee for a single attempt: lucky and unlucky streaks are normal. Legendary fish odds may be seeded or changed by the game, so check important decisions in-game. Wiki sources: Fishing, Drones.

How this comparison works

Both sides are expected bars per base ore, using the community Transmuter calculator's formulas. Bar level mode compares one ore tier. Void Drone floor mode starts with the floor's natural ore mix, applies portal upgrades, and compares the resulting mix.

  • Craft multiplier combines the free-craft chance with the 2x, 3x, 5x, 10x, 20x, and 100x craft chances.
  • Bomb of Plenty multiplies the affected ore, applies the natural and BoP golden-ore chances, then crafts all of that ore into bars.
  • Transmuter turns the affected ore directly into bars, can also apply a BoP stack, and the remaining ore is crafted normally.
  • Void Drone floors use the floor's 80/20 or 50/50 ore pair. Each native ore has the portal chance to become a portal; a portaled ore becomes one uniformly random ore from Tin up to its own level and is multiplied by the portal multiplier.
  • Breakpoint is the effective ore cost per bar where the two totals are equal. Cheaper bars favour Bomb of Plenty; more expensive bars favour Transmuter.
  • Bar output multiplier applies to bars from crafting and from the Transmuter Bomb, so it changes the bar totals but not the winner or breakpoint.

Bar costs use the wiki's current values through Neopolium (World 4). Effective cost is max(1, floor(base cost × craft-cost multiplier)), following the community calculator with a minimum of 1 ore per bar; that rounding can move a level right at the breakpoint to the other side. Floor mode is an aggregate bar-count proxy: a mixed floor produces several bar types, so use the native-ore rows for a specific target bar. Golden ore and BoP interactions are expected values, not guaranteed outcomes. Using both bombs together (for example with a spoon auto-firing both) is always better than using either one alone. 3. Estimate

How this estimate works

Goal ETA turns the amount still missing into time at your measured ore per hour. Everything is an estimate, not a promise.

  • Ore goal: ore remaining = max(0, target − current ore), then play hours = ore remaining ÷ ore per hour.
  • Bar goal: bars per ore = bar output multiplier × craft multiplier ÷ effective ore cost. The ore still to mine is the ore needed for the remaining bars, minus the ore you already have.
  • Calendar time: calendar days = play hours ÷ play hours per day. The finish date assumes you spread those hours evenly from now.
  • Deadline: available play time is the wall-clock time before the deadline scaled by your hours per day. The required rate is the ore remaining divided by that play time.
  • Milestones: 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100% of the target, with the same rate and hours-per-day assumptions.

Caveats: ore per hour varies by world, pickaxe, rock layout, upgrades, and how actively you play. Bar conversion ignores ore spent on other bars, bombs, or upgrades, and the craft multipliers are random, so short sessions can differ a lot from the average. A deadline assumes you actually play the hours you entered. Current ore and bar balances are not in ExportStats, so keep those fields up to date yourself.

How this planner works

Every contract upgrade is mapped to the goals it helps. The goal value is the product of its upgrades' factors, and a buy is scored by the weighted log gain it adds per Contract Point spent.

  • Additive multi: f = 1 + X × level. Contract bonuses are treated as their own factor, not added into the export's huge stat totals.
  • Chance: f = 1 + min(1, c0 + X × level) × (m − 1), where c0 = export chance − X × owned levels. A chance at 100% gains nothing and reads "Capped".
  • Craft: the triple and 10x craft chances are changed one at a time through the bomb tool's craft multiplier.
  • Bar cost: f = 1 / (1 − 0.05 × level).
  • Per contract: f = 1 + 0.01 × level × contracts completed.
  • Score: ∑ weight × ln(G(L+1) / G(L)) / cost. The plan spends greedily on the best affordable score until no positive buy fits.
  • Respec: budget = spare CP + the cost of every owned level. Owned levels are refunded at the current cost-reduction rate.

Costs use the wiki's CostTable values through 14 extra cap levels. The cost reduction is applied as cost × (1 − contract_upgrade_cost_reduction), which is an assumption worth checking against one in-game price. Portal and damage factors multiply the whole goal, which overstates them when only part of your income uses portals or when pickaxe/bomb use is not 50/50. The two damage branches are averaged 50/50. Greedy spend is not guaranteed to be the exact optimum.

How this calculator works

The calculator starts from your exported pickaxe and bomb stats, removes the consumables and damage contracts already included in that export, then rebuilds the numbers for the selected scenario.

  • Base damage: the export's pickaxe damage is divided by the current damage-contract factor and by the consumables that were active at export. The selected consumables and contract levels are then applied.
  • Consumables: Rock Cake (+30%) and Primal Meat (+100%, Golden +200%) add together; Hamburger multiplies that result (3.00–8.64x, Golden 6.00–17.28x). Apple, Pike, and Juicy Plums adjust crit chance and crit damage. Chaos Totem multiplies bomb damage. Banana Coffee and Blue Cow affect DPS, not the maximum hit.
  • Contracts: the eight damage upgrades are reset to their current cap. OB-locked upgrades stay at their current level. Pickaxe Damage, Pickaxe Crit Damage, the per-contract upgrades, and the 1.08x upgrade multiply the hit; the Super/Ultra/Omega crit chance upgrades decide which tiers can trigger. Non-damage contracts are unchanged, and Base Game Speed / Bomb Recharge are not maxed because they do not change a single hit's damage.
  • Crit chain: a Super crit needs a Crit, an Ultra needs a Super, and an Omega needs an Ultra. The maximum hit uses the highest tier with a non-zero chance. The expected hit weights every outcome by its probability.
  • DPS: expected hit × attack speed × game speed. Game speed is read from the export and may include the floor you exported on.

The export is treated as already including the contract levels and active items it records. If the game adds contract bonuses in a different way, the contract portion can differ. The Obelisk armour check compares the hit to the base armour formula only; ore damage-reduction modifiers are not applied. Verify important decisions in-game.

How this works

A port of the community sheet IOM – Best Floor To Farm (V3.4.2, game v2.2.52). Every floor gets a value per hour and floors are ranked by it.

  • Floor ores: floor f mixes ore round(f/2) + max(0, ⌊(f−13)/6⌋) and the next one, 80/20 on odd floors and 50/50 on even ones.
  • Floors per hour: 2880 × base game speed × floor speed modifier, with World 3 at 0.7× until the speed notice is bought and World 4 at 1 − quest modifier. The game speed in your export is divided by the penalties of the floor you exported on.
  • Floor multi: golden ore × ore income × golden floor × rainbow floor × galactic floor × prismatic floor, using the fixed golden-floor chance and modifiers of the “negative” floors (e.g. golden floors at 30–50%, Hattium / Mintium floors cutting portal chance to 0.4×).
  • Void Drone: a portal turns a rock into a random ore of equal or lower level, so it can reach your ore from any higher floor. Auto uses it when its best floor beats the best no-void floor.
  • Value: the share of a “perfect floor” where every ore on screen is yours. Values with and without the Void Drone use different perfect floors, so compare floors, not the percentages across modes.
  • Veins: veins per screen come from your spawn rate and the vein’s rarity (doubled for 2× research, capped at a full screen); void portals can also turn rocks into veins.

Differences from the sheet: stats are read from your saved account instead of the JSON tab, hand edits are kept as overrides, every reminder is shown (not just the first), the “can’t one-shot” check reports the real answer (the sheet’s text parser leaves a stray } on the last export key, so its cells show #VALUE! and then say “yes”), and Void Drone can be forced on or off. Warning: the sheet’s numbers assume the stats are exported on a floor with no modifiers.

Snapshots are stored in this browser. Download the history file to keep a backup or move it to another device. Dates use the export's recorded clock in your local timezone. Editing a date changes where it sits on the chart, not its export identity. Clear history does not reset your active account.

How the tracker works
  • Recording: every successful paste, file pick, or drag-drop of an ExportStats JSON appends a snapshot. The built-in sample exports are not recorded, and importing the same export twice does not create a duplicate.
  • Dates: the export's time is an Excel serial date, so it already contains the date and time the game made the file. Exports without a valid time fall back to the browser import time. You can correct a date in the snapshot table.
  • Backfilling: an export older than the account currently loaded into the other tools is recorded here but does not replace that account. Use Use in the snapshot table if you deliberately want to load an older one.
  • Graph: one stat is plotted in its own units. Selecting more than one stat switches to a normalized comparison where every series starts at 100% at its first positive value, because raw scales can differ by dozens of orders of magnitude. Tap or hover a point to read exact values, or open Chart data. Up to eight stats can be compared at once.
  • Storage: history lives in IndexedDB, separate from the active account in local storage. If IndexedDB is unavailable, the tracker falls back to local storage and keeps the newest 50 snapshots.
  • Backups: Download history writes a JSON bundle. Import history merges it by export date; an existing export date is replaced only when the incoming record differs.

A snapshot is a point-in-time copy of the exported stats, not a live save. Stats that the game changes constantly (ore, bars, gems) are not in ExportStats and are not tracked. The graph is a progress view, not a projection.
